# Team interview script

## Opening framing statement

"I am not here to ask whether you use AI. I want to know what you use to sort, score, rank, draft, summarise or decide anything, about a person, a case, or a piece of work. That includes spreadsheets, and it includes features already sitting inside tools you use every day. Nothing you tell me here changes what you can keep using, it just means we can support it properly."

## Questions

1. Walk me through a typical case or task from start to finish. At which points do you decide something, or does something get decided for you?
2. Is there anything in that walk-through that sorts, ranks, scores or filters before you see it?
3. Do you use a spreadsheet anywhere in this process to work out a score, an eligibility outcome, or a priority order?
4. Are there any features inside the systems you already use, your CRM, your case management tool, your HR platform, that suggest, rank, or flag things automatically? Have you ever turned one of those on, or had it turned on for you?
5. Do you use any browser extensions, desktop assistants, or add-ins to help with drafting, summarising or research?
6. Is there anything you use on your personal account, or a free version of a tool, because the corporate version was not available or was slower to get approved?
7. Has anyone on the team built or is anyone trialling anything that touches real cases or real customer data, even informally?
8. If a colleague reviews the output of any of these before it is used, how often do they actually change it? Roughly, not precisely.
9. Is there anything that runs, connects to something, or acts on your behalf without you checking each time it happens?
10. Who else on the team, or in an adjacent team, should I be asking these same questions?

## Notes for the interviewer

- Ask about tasks first, and only translate into "is this AI" afterwards. Leading with "do you use AI" reliably returns a low number, because most people do not think of a spreadsheet, a CRM feature, or a browser add-on as AI.
- Spreadsheets and embedded features are the two categories most often left out. Ask about them by name, do not assume question 1 will surface them.
- A vague or hedged answer to question 8 is itself useful information, not a gap. Record it as given.
- Question 10 is not a courtesy close. Follow every name it produces.
